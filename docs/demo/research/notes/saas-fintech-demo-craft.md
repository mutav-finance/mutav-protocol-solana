# Craft of Top SaaS and Fintech Product Demo and Launch Videos

Scope: patterns for a ≤3-minute recorded product demo for MUTAV (B2B fintech, Brazil's onchain rent guarantee). About 14 searches and fetches were run. Sourced material on how specific companies make their videos is thin. Most of what is citable comes from vendor blogs (Arcade, Supademo, Navattic, Consensus, TechSmith) and a few secondhand write-ups. The **Inferences** sections hold craft judgement that has no source; they are labelled as such.

## 1. How do best-in-class companies (Stripe, Linear, Ramp, Mercury, Brex, Browser Company, Vercel, Figma, Apple) structure a short product demo?

### Takeaway
The pattern that shows up again and again is this: a real, working product fills the frame. One job is shown per video. Narration is minimal, often from a founder or product lead. The value comes first, not as a slow build-up. Fintech brands (Mercury, Ramp) split their work in two: emotional "people behind the money" brand films, and separate plain product walkthroughs.

### Cited Findings
- **Linear, "Introducing Linear Initiatives" (June 25, 2024), 4:15.** The Head of Product (Nan) narrates from a small circular webcam bubble in the bottom-right corner while the real Linear UI fills the frame. The reviewer credits keeping the interface full-screen as the reason it works for a software audience. — [Moonb, 12 product launch videos](https://www.moonb.io/blog/product-launch-video)
- **Linear's short-form reels** run about 45–90 s each, use minimal narration, and show "a single job to be done per reel". This is a secondhand observation. — [Moonb / launch-video roundups via search](https://www.moonb.io/blog/product-launch-video); also [autoae.online roundup](https://autoae.online/blog/best-product-launch-videos)
- **Figma, "Introducing Figma Motion" (June 24, 2026), 1:09.** A motion-design reveal with no voiceover noted. The cursor moves among real designs, and a "Motion Tokens" panel (easing presets "Brand: Linear / Snappy / Playful") sits in the same frame as the animation it produces. In other words, the cause and the effect share one frame. — [Moonb](https://www.moonb.io/blog/product-launch-video); launch post [figma.com/blog/introducing-figma-motion](https://www.figma.com/blog/introducing-figma-motion/)
- **Stripe Sessions keynotes are live demos of purpose-built apps.** In 2026, the product keynote (Will Gaybrick, after Patrick Collison's opening) demoed an agentic app that reviews any public API for $2 per review. In 2025, an agent bought from a fictional skincare store, "GlowGuide", across three sites, and the presenter openly acknowledged a demo failure. — [Stripe Sessions 2026 keynote](https://stripe.com/sessions/2026/opening-remarks-and-product); [Stripe Sessions 2025 product keynote](https://stripe.com/sessions/2025/product-keynote)
- Stripe employs a dedicated **Demo Engineer** to "build the on-stage demos that anchor our product announcement keynotes". In practice the demo is a product in its own right, with a believable fictional merchant. — [Stripe jobs: Demo Engineer](https://stripe.com/jobs/listing/demo-engineer/7390314)
- A 2026 critique of Stripe's keynote slides says they pass a "ten-second test", with minimal text that leaves the speaker to supply the meaning. This is a single blog's opinion. — [StrategyU](https://strategyu.co/stripe-sessions-2026-keynote-communication/)
- **The Browser Company (Arc/Dia)** fronts its launches with the CEO (Josh Miller) showing early prototypes on concrete tasks, such as typing commands into the address bar. It anchors them with a single thesis line ("AI won't exist as an app. Or a button… an entirely new environment"). Founder video letters are a recurring format. — [Maginative](https://www.maginative.com/article/dia-is-a-new-ai-browser-from-the-makers-of-arc/); [Every podcast](https://every.to/podcast/inside-the-browser-company-why-they-killed-arc-to-build-dia)
- **Mercury** opened its Series C with a story-led film instead of a press release. Brand director Heather MacKinnon calls "evoking emotion through brand storytelling while still communicating what our product does" one of the hardest balances in brand work. Script, music, sound design and illustration were done in-house; the animation studio was Scholar. — [Lorikeet interview with Heather MacKinnon](https://www.lorikeetcx.ai/news/great-brand-extends-beyond-marketing-heather-mackinnon-mercury); [Instrument case study](https://www.instrument.com/work/mercury)
- A third-party analysis says Mercury's video work centres on relatable founder conversations about outcomes, not feature lists. This is one agency's interpretation. — [Zelios](https://zelios.agency/mercury-brand-video-strategy/)
- **Ramp's "Love Finance"** (its first brand platform, agency Arts & Letters, director Jara Moravec) aims to make finance "meaningful by spotlighting the people behind the numbers". Ramp's product marketing job postings ask for "storyboards, demo scripts" and launch content that makes "product news fun and memorable". — [DesignRush](https://news.designrush.com/ramp-and-arts-letters-new-ad-series); [Ramp job posting](https://jobs.coatue.com/companies/ramp-2/jobs/25954181-senior-technical-product-marketing-manager)
- **"Do the Last Thing First" (Peter Cohan, *Great Demo!*).** Open with the end result and the most impressive capability, not a build-up. Guy Kawasaki, crediting Cohan: you have about a minute to captivate, so "don't try building to a crescendo. Start with 'shock and awe'". — [Pragmatic Institute webinar](https://pragmaticinstitute.com/resources/webinars/product/create-and-deliver-surprisingly-compelling-software-demonstrations); [SK Murphy on Great Demo!](https://www.skmurphy.com/blog/2014/02/20/improve-your-sales-pitch/)
- **Hook in the first 5–10 s.** Supademo says the first 10 seconds should state the viewer's problem, not introduce the product, then say early what the product does and for whom. Script templates use the order hook (≤5 s) → problem → solution, and warn against spending the first 10 s on menus and settings. — [Supademo demo script guide](https://supademo.com/blog/demo-script); [JoinBrands script guide](https://joinbrands.com/blog/product-demo-video-script/)
- Motion The Agency says strong demos open with the problem, not the product. — [Motion The Agency](https://www.motiontheagency.com/blog/best-product-demo-videos-that-convert-with-expert-tips)
- **Ending and CTA.** Navattic's top-performing demos "start with a hook, address customers directly", and end with a call to action. — [Navattic docs](https://docs.navattic.com/build-your-demo)
- **YC Demo Day** gives each company **one minute**. That is a forcing function for one idea, stated plainly. — [YC S20 Virtual Demo Day, Michael Seibel](https://blog.ycombinator.com/yc-s20-virtual-demo-day); [YC S21](https://blog.ycombinator.com/yc-s21-virtual-demo-day/)

### Inferences
- A structure for MUTAV's ≤3:00, combining Cohan's "last thing first", Linear's "one job per video" and Stripe's "demo as a product with a believable fictional customer". Timings are craft judgement, not sourced:
  - **0:00–0:08, the hook as end state.** Open on the result: a landlord's screen showing "Contrato garantido" (contract guaranteed) and the reserve coverage live, or the agency approving a tenant with no fiador (guarantor) and no caução (deposit). One line of VO or on-screen text naming the pain, e.g. "In Brazil, renting still means a guarantor, a 3-month deposit, or an expensive seguro-fiança (rent-guarantee insurance)."
  - **0:08–0:25, the problem in one sentence plus the promise.** No logo sting longer than about 1 s.
  - **0:25–2:20, one user, one flow.** Follow one persona (e.g. a real-looking agency, "Imobiliária Exemplo", with tenant "Ana") through 3–4 beats: issue the guarantee → payment and default event → claim paid from the reserve → transparency/coverage proof onchain. Each beat ends on a visible state change.
  - **2:20–2:45, proof.** The onchain verification and the coverage ratio, which is the fintech trust beat.
  - **2:45–3:00, CTA.** One action only (e.g. "reserve.mutav.finance", or "Talk to us"), with the logo held 2–3 s.
- Fintech leaders keep the emotional brand film apart from the product walkthrough. For a 3-minute demo, borrow one human touch (a named tenant, a real-looking rental) but keep it a product video.
- Stripe's acknowledged live failure argues for recording, not presenting live, under a hackathon deadline.

### Gaps
- No primary or analysed source was found for **Vercel Ship**, **Brex**, **Apple feature films** or **Ramp/Mercury product (not brand) demos**: no timings, shot lists or structure breakdowns. Linear's own production process (tools, team) is undocumented publicly.
- Lenny's Newsletter, a16z and First Round Review articles specifically on recorded product demos were not found within the search budget.

## 2. Screen-recording polish: auto-zoom, cursor smoothing, resolution and frame rate, browser chrome, fake vs real data, click pacing, holds, cutting waits

### Takeaway
The "Screen Studio aesthetic" is auto-zoom to the click target, a smoothed synthetic cursor, motion blur, a padded background, and idle time sped up or cut. Cursor discipline matters more than any effect: move with purpose, then park the cursor. Use zooms and callouts, not cursor-wiggling, for emphasis. Cut every load and every bit of typing.

### Cited Findings
- Screen Studio automatically applies smooth cursor movement, auto-zoom on clicks and motion blur, and puts the recording on a background. One user's workflow is record → stop → about 2 min of output settings → export. — [Dave Swift review](https://daveswift.com/screen-studio/)
- Competitor Smooth Recorder offers the same set: zoom where you click, smooth the cursor, add captions, place on a background. Zoom timing is fine-tuned in its editor afterwards. — [Product Hunt: Smooth Recorder](https://www.producthunt.com/posts/smooth-recorder)
- An open-source alternative implements the aesthetic as discrete operations: auto speed-up of idle segments, auto-zoom on click clusters, keystroke overlay chips, a smoothed synthetic cursor, and easing curves (Smooth / Snappy / Linear) with a smoothness slider, via ffmpeg. — [screenstudio-alt skill](https://claudskills.com/skills/screenstudio-alt/SKILL.md)
- **Cursor:** "Lots of mouse movement can make the viewer feel disoriented." Navigate, then leave the cursor still or move it to a neutral spot. — [Consensus, 5 tips](https://goconsensus.com/blog/5-crucial-tips-to-recording-the-best-product-video)
- "Viewers' eyes go to anything that moves, and the point-with-the-mouse habit that works in live demos is very distracting on video. Callouts, zooms, and pans are better tools for emphasis." — [TechSmith via search](https://www.techsmith.com/blog/edit-demo-recording/)
- **Record in chunks:** one feature or capability per take. Slow loads and typing get fixed in post with pause, zoom, speed-up and cross-fade, so "don't worry about how long you spend on each screen" while recording. — [Consensus](https://goconsensus.com/blog/5-crucial-tips-to-recording-the-best-product-video)
- **Edit order:** cut mistakes, pauses and dead air *first*, before effects, branding or captions, then even out the audio. — [TechSmith](https://www.techsmith.com/blog/edit-demo-recording/)
- **Framing:** choose a 16:9 window size and record an area slightly larger than the product window, because cropping later is easier than re-recording. — [Consensus](https://goconsensus.com/blog/5-crucial-tips-to-recording-the-best-product-video)
- Hide desktop icons and turn off notifications so badges and sounds don't land in the take. — [ScreenPal tips](https://screenpal.com/blog/screen-recording-tips); [wowto.ai](https://wowto.ai/blog/top-5-mistakes-to-avoid-while-recording-your-screen)
- Figma Motion's launch keeps the control and its effect in the same frame (the Motion Tokens panel beside the animating design). That framing choice spares the viewer from having to remember what was clicked. — [Moonb](https://www.moonb.io/blog/product-launch-video)
- **Real vs fake data, from the leaders:** Stripe builds believable fictional merchants for demos (GlowGuide; a $2 API-review app). — [Stripe Sessions 2025](https://stripe.com/sessions/2025/product-keynote); [2026](https://stripe.com/sessions/2026/opening-remarks-and-product)
- **Specific numbers beat vague claims** in demo copy (Arcade, 14M sessions). — [Arcade 2026 Benchmarks](https://www.arcade.software/post/2026-arcade-benchmarks)

### Inferences
These are unsourced but standard among practitioners. Verify them against your own test renders.
- Record at a 2× (Retina) source, e.g. a 1440×900 logical browser window at 2880×1800, and export 1080p or 1440p at **60 fps** for smooth cursor and zoom motion. Use 30 fps only if file size forces it. Do not record at 4K with UI text left tiny; set browser zoom so body text is at least about 16 px on a 1080p export.
- Hide browser chrome. Either record a chromeless window (an app or kiosk window, or Screen Studio's window capture with the background) or crop it. If a URL must show (e.g. `reserve.mutav.finance` as a CTA), show it once on purpose.
- Data: use **fictional but plausible, locale-correct data**. BRL formatting (R$ 2.450,00), Brazilian names, São Paulo addresses, realistic rents, and round-but-not-too-round numbers. Never show real tenant PII. This also matches MUTAV's on-chain data-minimisation stance (opaque IDs). Pre-seed every state so that no step waits on the network.
- Pacing: about 1 click per 2–4 s of video. After a meaningful result (guarantee issued, claim paid), **hold 1.5–3 s**, zoomed in, before the next action. Cut every wait for wallet signing or block confirmation to under 0.5 s, or show a sped-up "1×→8×" moment, so the viewer still sees that it is onchain without having to sit through the wait.
- Zoom: zoom in only for the 2–4 key moments; constant zooming tires the viewer. Use ease-in-out over about 0.4–0.7 s. Zoom out between beats so the viewer re-orients.

### Gaps
- No sourced numbers were found for recommended resolution, frame rate, zoom duration, hold time, or clicks per minute. The figures above are inference.
- No source compares real against fake data for demo-video credibility.

## 3. Voiceover, music and captions: words per minute, script style, human vs AI voice, music level, captions for silent autoplay

### Takeaway
Plan narration at about **140–150 wpm**, so roughly **400–430 words for a 3:00 video**, and fewer if the visuals need breathing room. Write in second person, present tense, short sentences. Captions are mandatory because a large share of social and feed video plays muted. AI voiceover showed *higher* completion in Arcade's interactive-demo data, but that data is from click-through demos, not a 3-minute film.

### Cited Findings
- Narration pace: one studio's voice actors run about 150 wpm, and its 60 s scripts are 140–150 words. — [Bread n Beyond](https://breadnbeyond.com/how-many-words-does-a-60-seconds-explainer-video-needs/)
- Other planning rates: 140 wpm (70 words per 30 s, 140 per 60 s, 210 per 90 s, 280 per 2 min); 130–150 wpm; 125–150 wpm; Common Craft about 160 wpm. All are vendor rules of thumb. Read aloud with a timer. — [YumYum Videos](https://www.yumyumvideos.com/blog/short-vs-long-videos-explainer-video-length-wp/); [truScribe](https://truscribe.com/feeds/blog/explainer-video-script-template); [Stimme24](https://www.stimme24.com/en/guide/explainer-video-script/); [Common Craft](https://commoncraft.com/explainer-tip-writing-explainer-video-script)
- **Silent viewing:** up to 85% of Facebook video views happen with sound off, according to publishers (LittleThings 85%; PopSugar 50–80%). Agency MEC reported 85–90% silent views on branded video, with brand lift unaffected by sound on or off. These are publisher-reported figures, not a formal study, and the frequent attribution to "Facebook internal data" is unsupported. — [Digiday](https://digiday.com/media/silent-world-facebook-video/)
- **AI voiceover:** interactive demos with AI-generated voiceover show **14% higher completion**. Arcade advises concise narration paced to the visuals, and letting users mute it. — [Arcade 2026 Benchmarks](https://www.arcade.software/post/2026-arcade-benchmarks)
- **A human presence helps:** demos with a personal camera intro reach >55% completion, and tutorial-style demos with camera throughout reach >60%. — [Arcade 2026 Benchmarks](https://www.arcade.software/post/2026-arcade-benchmarks)
- Linear uses a named product lead in a webcam bubble. The Browser Company fronts launches with its CEO. — [Moonb](https://www.moonb.io/blog/product-launch-video); [Maginative](https://www.maginative.com/article/dia-is-a-new-ai-browser-from-the-makers-of-arc/)
- **On-screen copy:** keep labels under 60 characters (26–59 is the target); going over 60 cut completion by 12%. Phrasing as questions raised clicks about 40%. One or two ALL-CAPS words per block raised engagement 20%. Specific numbers beat vague claims. These figures are from interactive demos. — [Arcade 2026 Benchmarks](https://www.arcade.software/post/2026-arcade-benchmarks)
- Navattic: dialog copy of about 25–30 words, "inviting and personal", addressing the customer directly. — [Navattic docs](https://docs.navattic.com/build-your-demo)
- Mercury made its own music and sound design in-house for its brand film, which suggests audio is treated as brand, not stock filler. — [Lorikeet](https://www.lorikeetcx.ai/news/great-brand-extends-beyond-marketing-heather-mackinnon-mercury)

### Inferences
- MUTAV word budget: **3:00 at about 140 wpm ≈ 420 words maximum.** Aim for about 330–380 so there is room for 2–3 silent "hold on the result" beats. The hook (≤8 s) gets about 15–20 words.
- Script style: second person, present tense, active verbs, with one idea per sentence ("You approve Ana in minutes. No fiador. No caução."). Name things the way the UI names them, so VO and screen agree.
- Language: a judging or investor audience (Colosseum) wants English VO plus burned-in English captions. For the Brazilian agency audience, cut a pt-BR version. Either way, hardcode captions (open captions) for muted autoplay on X and LinkedIn, and also ship an SRT for YouTube.
- Caption typography (unsourced craft): 1–2 lines, about 32–42 characters per line, sentence case, in the brand sans at roughly 4–5% of frame height, high contrast with a subtle shadow or plate. Place them in the lower third and keep them clear of UI areas being demonstrated. Use kinetic "title cards" (big type, 1–2 s) only for the hook, beat headers and CTA.
- Music: an instrumental bed ducked to about −18 to −24 LUFS-relative under the VO (roughly 15–20 dB below the voice). Bring it up only in no-VO transitions and at the end card. Loudness target about −14 LUFS integrated for YouTube and social. This is standard audio practice, not from a source here.
- Human vs AI voice: Arcade's +14% is for click-through demos. A founder voice carries more credibility for a hackathon or B2B fintech trust pitch, in line with the Linear and Browser Company practice. A good compromise is a short founder face or VO intro, then a clean VO for the walkthrough.

### Gaps
- No source gave measured music-to-VO ratios for top SaaS launch videos.
- No head-to-head study of human vs AI voice was found for *linear* (non-interactive) product videos.
- No caption-typography study was found for product demos specifically.

## 4. Patterns from demo-craft experts (Great Demo!, YC, a16z / First Round, Arcade / Supademo / Navattic)

### Takeaway
The experts agree on four things. Lead with the end result. Keep it to one problem for one persona. Front-load value before the attention cliff (about step 7, or about 60 s). Close with one CTA. Length benchmarks for long-form demos cluster at 1–3 min (YouTube) and 2–5 min (explainers), with 60–90 s cuts for social.

### Cited Findings
- **Great Demo! (Peter Cohan):** "Do the Last Thing First", meaning show the end result first, turning the demo "upside down". The book dates from 2003, with a 2nd edition in 2005 that added remote demos. — [Pragmatic Institute](https://pragmaticinstitute.com/resources/webinars/product/create-and-deliver-surprisingly-compelling-software-demonstrations); [SK Murphy](https://www.skmurphy.com/blog/2014/02/20/improve-your-sales-pitch/)
- **Arcade, 2026 Benchmarks (14M interactive sessions).** Completion by step count: 1–5 steps 22%; 6–8 steps 32%; **9–12 steps 40%** (the peak); 13–15 steps 37%; 16–20 steps 28%; 21–30 steps 40% (depth-seekers). **Step 7 is the main drop-off**, and viewers who pass it are 2.3× likelier to finish. Steps 1–6 are "decision steps", so put core value there. Video share: 0% video 41% completion, falling to 21% at 51–100% video. Use video as an accent, in 1–3 short bursts. — [Arcade](https://www.arcade.software/post/2026-arcade-benchmarks)
- Arcade: explainer videos ideally 2–5 min; a polished 60–90 s B2B demo can be produced in 30–90 minutes. — [Arcade, AI video storytelling](https://arcade.software/post/ai-video-product-storytelling); [Arcade, production time](https://www.arcade.software/post/how-ai-video-generation-reduces-demo-production-time)
- Length by channel: YouTube 60 s–3 min; product pages 30–120 s (split multi-use-case demos into 30–45 s clips); paid ads 6–30 s. — [Racklify](https://racklify.com/encyclopedia/product-demo-video-lengths-and-formats-how-to-optimize-for-youtube-ads-and-product-pages/)
- Top-of-funnel social demos work best under 60–90 s. — [Framia](https://framia.converge.ai/page/en-US/blog/product-demo-video-tips)
- **Supademo:** the first 10 s must show you understand the viewer's problem. Don't open with a product intro. Say what it does and for whom, early. — [Supademo](https://supademo.com/blog/demo-script)
- **Navattic:** top demos have a hook, address the customer directly, use 10–15 steps of 100–200 characters each, and end with a CTA. — [Navattic docs](https://docs.navattic.com/build-your-demo)
- **YC:** Demo Day is one minute per company. — [YC blog](https://blog.ycombinator.com/yc-s20-virtual-demo-day)

### Inferences
- Map Arcade's "step 7 cliff" to video: treat the first about 45–60 s as the decision window. By then the viewer must have seen the problem, the end result, and the first real product action.
- Arcade's 9–12-step sweet spot suggests MUTAV's flow should be about 9–12 *visible actions* in total across all beats, not 25.
- Prepare a 60–90 s cutdown of the same footage for X and LinkedIn alongside the 3:00 master.

### Gaps
- No a16z, First Round Review or Lenny's Newsletter article specifically on recorded product demo videos was retrieved within budget. Their pitch-deck and demo-day advice exists but was not verified here.
- No YC source specifically on demo *videos* (as opposed to the Demo Day format) was found.

## 5. Common mistakes that make product demos feel amateur

### Takeaway
The amateur tells are: opening with logos, menus or settings instead of the pain; a wandering cursor; visible waits and typing; real clutter (notifications, bookmarks, desktop icons); too many features; video-heavy padding; long on-screen text; and no single CTA.

### Cited Findings
- Opening on menus and settings instead of the pain, or with a product introduction instead of the problem. — [JoinBrands](https://joinbrands.com/blog/product-demo-video-script/); [Supademo](https://supademo.com/blog/demo-script)
- Building to a climax instead of leading with the strongest result. — [SK Murphy / Kawasaki on Cohan](https://www.skmurphy.com/blog/2014/02/20/improve-your-sales-pitch/)
- Pointing and wiggling with the mouse, which disorients viewers. — [Consensus](https://goconsensus.com/blog/5-crucial-tips-to-recording-the-best-product-video); [TechSmith](https://www.techsmith.com/blog/edit-demo-recording/)
- Leaving in dead air, loading delays, typing and mistakes, and doing effects before the cut. — [TechSmith](https://www.techsmith.com/blog/edit-demo-recording/); [Consensus](https://goconsensus.com/blog/5-crucial-tips-to-recording-the-best-product-video)
- Notifications and desktop clutter in the frame. — [ScreenPal](https://screenpal.com/blog/screen-recording-tips); [wowto.ai](https://wowto.ai/blog/top-5-mistakes-to-avoid-while-recording-your-screen)
- Cropping the capture too tight, which leaves nothing to reframe in post. — [Consensus](https://goconsensus.com/blog/5-crucial-tips-to-recording-the-best-product-video)
- Over-long copy (>60 characters per label cut completion 12%), vague claims instead of specific numbers, and too many steps (16–20 steps is the completion trough). — [Arcade 2026 Benchmarks](https://www.arcade.software/post/2026-arcade-benchmarks)
- Over-using video in interactive demos (51–100% video means 21% completion vs 41% with none). — [Arcade](https://www.arcade.software/post/2026-arcade-benchmarks)
- Trying to show everything instead of one job per video. — [Moonb on Linear](https://www.moonb.io/blog/product-launch-video)

### Inferences
These are unsourced craft observations:
- Placeholder or lorem data, US-formatted currency in a Brazilian product, "test123" names, or empty states visible in a hero shot.
- Mismatched VO and screen: the narrator says "approve" while the button says "Confirmar".
- Background music louder than the VO, or a track with lyrics.
- Inconsistent zoom levels, jittery zoom-in/zoom-out on every click, and differing window sizes between takes.
- Low-resolution or 30 fps cursor motion, compression artefacts on gradients, and a visible scrollbar or dev tools.
- Ending on a fade to black with no URL, or with three CTAs.
- Explaining the blockchain before the user benefit. For MUTAV, onchain proof should be the trust *payoff* near the end, not the opening.

### Gaps
- No quantitative study of which mistakes most damage credibility in fintech or B2B demo videos specifically.
