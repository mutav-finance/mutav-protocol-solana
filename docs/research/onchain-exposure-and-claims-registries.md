# Copy the ledgers, not the governance mistakes

*Research date: 2026-10-10.*

No public blockchain runs a live registry of rental guarantees today. MUTAV's per-lease fiança ledger (cover granted, claims filed, claim payments made from a pooled reserve) would be the first. The parts it needs do exist, though, spread across three families. **Cover protocols** (Nexus Mutual, Etherisc, Sherlock) show how recorded exposure can drive a capital requirement inside the same transaction. **On-chain credit registries** (Centrifuge, Maple, Goldfinch, Huma, Credix) show how to recognise losses before final default and how to reverse them under control. **Bank-guarantee networks** (Lygon in Australia, whose first use case was guarantees on retail property leases) show that landlords value instant, verifiable guarantee status. Every mature design settles on the same shape: a compact record per exposure, keyed by an id or a hash, with an explicit state enum, amounts split by type, and documents behind a hash. Aggregate totals are updated in the same instruction that changes a record, and loss recognition only moves down unless an authority reverses it. The failures came from governance, keys and adoption, not from the ledgers. Claim terms were changed in the middle of events, token-vote assessors were captured, single keys held bundled admin roles, products were deprecated with live positions, and bank consortia never reached enough users. MUTAV's design already follows the core of these lessons: an operator/admin split, provisions for filed claims counted in coverage_required, PDAs seeded by opaque ids and hashes, and personal data kept off-chain. This report sets out what to add to that design and what to avoid.

## No public chain records rental guarantees today

The closest live precedents record either money movements or ownership, not guarantee obligations. Keyhold, a peer project in the same Colosseum hackathon, records rental-deposit and rent transfers on Solana devnet. Each transfer carries a memo of the form `lease:v1:<leaseId>:deposit:<sha256hex>`, so only hashes, amounts, timestamps and public keys go on-chain. Its Anchor escrow program is built and CI-tested but **not deployed**, and as of 2026-10-04 it reports **no users, pilots or LOIs** ([Keyhold](https://github.com/Malejo01/keyhold)). Real-estate tokenizers on Solana such as Homebase, which tokenized a $235k Texas rental for 38 investors in 2023, distribute rent to owners and record nothing about guarantees ([Solana case study](https://solana.com/news/case-study-homebase)). The 2017–2019 rent platforms (Rentberry, Rentible, Midasium) appear dormant. DeFi cover as a whole protects only about **0.5% of DeFi TVL** ([Coin Bureau](https://coinbureau.com/education/defi-insurance)), and targeted searches found no DeFi pool for rent guarantees or deposit replacement.

The strongest precedent for "this has been done for leases" is permissioned. Lygon was founded by ANZ, Commonwealth Bank, Westpac, IBM and Scentre Group, the landlord behind Westfield. In a July 2019 pilot with more than 20 businesses it issued digital bank guarantees for retail leases, and issuance time fell from **up to a month to about the same day**, with amendments and cancellations handled on the ledger ([CBA](https://www.commbank.com.au/guidance/newsroom/cba-lygon-blockchain-201907.html); [iTnews](https://www.itnews.com.au/news/lygon-records-first-australian-digital-bank-guarantee-560835)). It became a commercial company in September 2020 ([IBM AU](https://au.newsroom.ibm.com/2020-09-01-ANZ-Commonwealth-Bank-IBM-Scentre-Group-and-Westpac-expand-Lygon-platform)). Its status after 2021 is unknown. Bank Hapoalim built a similar guarantee network on Azure in 2017 and handed it to TCS in 2020 ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2017/09/07/microsoft-bank-hapoalim-blockchain-bank-guarantees); [Calcalist](https://www.calcalistech.com/articles/0,7340,L-3879850,00.html)). These ledgers held the instrument's lifecycle state (issue, amend, cancel, demand) and kept documents and identities in permissioned channels. That lifecycle is MUTAV's cover → claim → claim payment, with two differences: MUTAV's state is public, and it is backed by a pooled reserve rather than one bank's balance sheet.

Brazil runs its guarantee and receivables stack through authorised registries, not blockchains. Since 2 August 2021, Circular SUSEP 624/2021 has required every *seguro fiança locatícia* policy to be registered with an accredited registradora such as B3, CSD BR or Núclea ([Revista Cobertura](https://www.revistacobertura.com.br/noticias/seguros-gerais/b3-e-pottencial-seguradora-realizam-o-1o-registro-de-fianca-locaticia-junto-a-susep/); [SUSEP](https://www.gov.br/susep/pt-br/assuntos/sistema-de-registro-de-operacoes/registradoras)). CERC, a registry authorised by the Central Bank, accepts rent receivables and records their ownership, maturity, assignments and liens ([CERC manual](https://www.cerc.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/CERC_44-Manual-de-Ativos-CIF.pdf)). Drex tested a tokenized sale of a financed property in phase 2 and then **shelved DLT** for its next phase, because privacy and bank secrecy could not be reconciled with programmability. Its near-term deliverable is lien reconciliation, without DLT ([Seu Dinheiro](https://www.seudinheiro.com/2025/economia/drex-abandonara-blockchain-na-proxima-fase-para-entregar-solucao-em-2026-diz-jornal-gmda/)). For MUTAV, the on-chain registry is a transparency and audit layer that sits beside these legal registries, not in place of them. Whether any of them applies to a fiança product is a legal question outside this research.

## Every mature registry settles on one compact record shape

Across these families, the record per exposure is small and typed. Nexus Mutual's `CoverData` is `productId, coverAsset, amount, start, period, gracePeriod, rewardsRatio, capacityRatio`, keyed by a sequential id that is also the ERC-721 token id. The capital backing each cover sits beside it as `PoolAllocation[]`, so **one exposure can be split across several capital sources, and each part is decremented independently** on payout ([ICover.sol](https://github.com/NexusMutual/smart-contracts/blob/master/contracts/interfaces/ICover.sol)). This is the cleanest model for MUTAV's two-leg absolute cover: one guarantee record holding two sub-amounts, each with its own counter for provisions and claim payments. Etherisc keeps running counters on the record itself (`claimsCount, openClaimsCount, payoutMaxAmount, payoutAmount`), so a reader can see open and paid totals per exposure without scanning claims ([IPolicy.sol](https://github.com/etherisc/gif-interface/blob/HEAD/contracts/modules/IPolicy.sol)). Credit registries add the same split by type. Centrifuge records repayments as `{principal, interest, unscheduled}` ([types/mod.rs](https://github.com/centrifuge/centrifuge-chain/blob/main/pallets/loans/src/types/mod.rs)), and Huma separates principal, yield and fees in its `CreditRecord` and `DueDetail` ([CreditStructs.sol](https://github.com/00labs/huma-contracts-v2/blob/main/contracts/credit/CreditStructs.sol)).

Lifecycles are explicit enums with whitelisted transitions. Etherisc uses `ApplicationState {Applied, Revoked, Underwritten, Declined}`, `PolicyState {Active, Expired, Closed}`, `ClaimState {Applied, Confirmed, Declined, Closed}` and `PayoutState {Expected, PaidOut}` ([IPolicy.sol](https://github.com/etherisc/gif-interface/blob/HEAD/contracts/modules/IPolicy.sol)). Huma has both `CreditState {Deleted, Approved, GoodStanding, Delayed, Defaulted}` and an eight-state receivable enum ([CreditStructs.sol](https://github.com/00labs/huma-contracts-v2/blob/main/contracts/credit/CreditStructs.sol)). Centrifuge goes further and stores each phase in a separate map: `CreatedLoan`, `ActiveLoan` (iterable per pool and capped by `MaxActiveLoansPerPool`) and `ClosedLoan`, which is kept "for historical purposes. No mutations are expected" ([lib.rs](https://github.com/centrifuge/centrifuge-chain/blob/main/pallets/loans/src/lib.rs)). Nexus handles amendments by **creating a new cover id linked through `CoverReference {originalCoverId, latestCoverId}`**, which keeps history append-only and the current state one pointer away ([ICover.sol](https://github.com/NexusMutual/smart-contracts/blob/master/contracts/interfaces/ICover.sol)). That pattern suits rent adjustments and renewals.

Idempotent creation is a solved problem. Huma mints a receivable only after hashing `keccak256(contract, referenceId, creator)` into `referenceIdHashToTokenId` and rejecting duplicates, so the same off-chain invoice cannot be registered twice ([Receivable.sol](https://github.com/00labs/huma-contracts-v2/blob/main/contracts/credit/Receivable.sol)). On Solana the equivalent costs nothing. A PDA seeded by the hash of the external reference cannot be initialised twice, because Anchor's `init` fails if the account already exists ([Solana program-security course](https://github.com/solana-foundation/developer-content/tree/main/content/courses/program-security)). MUTAV's claim PDAs seeded by notice hash already use this. The consequence is that a mistaken filing permanently occupies its address. The record therefore needs terminal states such as `Withdrawn` and `Rejected` rather than deletion, and the hash should include a domain separator and a version byte so that schemas never collide.

On storage, plain per-record PDAs are the right choice at MUTAV's scale. A 200-byte account costs about 2.28M lamports at the original 6,960 lamports per byte. SIMD-0437 cuts that rate **90% to 696 in five steps**: step 1 went live on mainnet on 2026-09-03, and steps 3–5 are expected with Agave 4.4 around November 2026 ([Solana: Reduced Rent](https://solana.com/upgrades/reduced-rent)). At the final rate, 10,000 guarantees lock roughly 2.3 SOL (derived from the formula). ZK-compressed PDAs are about **100x cheaper to create** ([ZK Compression](https://www.zkcompression.com/)). The cost is about 200k CU of proof and hashing overhead per transaction, a dependency on the Photon indexer for every read and write, and records that other programs cannot read without proofs supplied by the caller. Light Protocol itself advises using regular accounts for data that is updated often or needed whole in one transaction ([ZK Compression: Considerations](https://www.zkcompression.com/learn/considerations.md)). Compression only becomes worth it in the hundreds of thousands of records. Keeping records open as tombstones, as MUTAV plans, is the safe default: closing a PDA by draining its lamports allows the same seeds to be reinitialised ([RareSkills](https://www.rareskills.io/post/solana-close-account)). If rent recovery ever matters, shrinking a tombstoned record with `realloc` keeps its address occupied and returns most of the lamports. That is an inferred design and needs testing against Anchor's `realloc` constraint.

## Exposure feeds the capital requirement inside the same transaction

The closest precedent for MUTAV's path from registry to coverage_required to coverage gate is Nexus Mutual, which treats exposure and capital as one pipeline. Every buy, edit, expiry and payout updates a global `totalActiveCoverInAsset` per asset. It does this incrementally, through **7-day expiry buckets** (`activeCoverExpirationBuckets`), instead of iterating over covers ([Cover.sol](https://github.com/NexusMutual/smart-contracts/blob/master/contracts/modules/cover/Cover.sol)). The capital requirement follows mechanically. "MCR = Total Active Cover Amount in ETH / Gearing Factor", with a gearing factor of **4.8**. The gearing factor is calibrated by running "the full capital model … off-chain" and is changed by governance if the two diverge ([Nexus docs: MCR](https://docs.nexusmutual.io/protocol/capital-pool/mcr)). Each payout forces an immediate recompute (`sendPayout` → `_updateMCR(true)`) ([Pool.sol](https://github.com/NexusMutual/smart-contracts/blob/master/contracts/modules/capital/Pool.sol)). Capacity is gated per staking pool at stake × 2.0 leverage. The Advisory Board can reduce capacity on any listing whose active cover approaches **20% of the minimum capital requirement**, which is a concentration control built into the parameter set ([Nexus docs: Capacity](https://docs.nexusmutual.io/protocol/capacity)).

Two parts of the Nexus design should be read carefully. First, its stored requirement moves toward the target by at most **500 bps per day and 100 bps per update** ([Pool.sol](https://github.com/NexusMutual/smart-contracts/blob/master/contracts/modules/capital/Pool.sol)). That smoothing protects a token price from jumps, but it lets the requirement lag behind a sudden rise in exposure. A coverage gate should read the unsmoothed value, and MUTAV's gate does exactly that. Second, Nexus counts only active cover against capital; claims still under assessment add nothing until they are paid. MUTAV's model **adds provisions for filed claims to coverage_required**, which is stricter than Nexus. It is closer to a reserve for reported claims, and to Maple's `unrealizedLosses` described below.

Etherisc GIF shows the simplest auditable alternative: a hard check at underwriting. Each risk pool locks `collateralizationLevel × sumInsured` when a policy is underwritten and releases it on close. Underwriting reverts unless `sumOfSumInsuredCap >= sumOfSumInsuredAtRisk + sumInsuredAmount`, and withdrawals require `pool.capital >= amount` ([IPool.sol](https://github.com/etherisc/gif-interface/blob/HEAD/contracts/modules/IPool.sol); [PoolController.sol](https://github.com/etherisc/gif-contracts/blob/HEAD/contracts/modules/PoolController.sol)). Both Nexus and Etherisc run the check in the same transaction that writes the exposure. That is the property worth copying: no gap in time between registering a guarantee and the reserve being tested against it.

On Solana the same discipline means that every instruction which changes a guarantee or claim takes the state account as `mut` and adjusts the cached totals with checked arithmetic. The invariant "sum of open records equals cached total" then holds by construction, and the coverage gate stays an O(1) read. An amend that changes a record's amount without applying the same delta to the total is the classic way totals drift. Two cheap additions guard against it (both are inferred designs; no audit report citing drift in cached totals was found). The first is to store the amount each record actually contributed (`counted_amount`), so that a release subtracts exactly what was added even if parameters changed in between. The second is a permissionless `verify_totals` crank over `remaining_accounts`. It recomputes the sum and emits a mismatch event or sets a pause flag, but never rewrites the totals, so the gate does not depend on a crank running. Kamino's `refresh_obligation` shows the shape of an ordered `remaining_accounts` recompute stamped with `last_update` ([klend docs mirror](https://www.mintlify.com/kamino-finance/klend/reference/instructions/obligation)). MUTAV needs a refresh-before-use rule only for inputs priced from the market, which here means the reserve side, not BRL-denominated guarantees. If the registry later moves to per-agency or aggregate disclosure, a **Merkle-sum tree** commits to a total while letting each party verify its own inclusion. OtterSec's PoRv2 adds ZK proofs of the sum and non-negativity to stop an operator leaving records out ([OtterSec](https://osec.io/blog/how-proof-of-reserves-uses-zk-to-protect-your-funds)).

## Claims and write-downs move down freely and up only under authority

Claim records are small structs keyed to the exposure, with evidence kept off-chain. Nexus's `Claim` is `{coverId, amount, coverAsset, payoutRedemptionPeriod, payoutRedeemed, depositRetrieved}` plus a `bytes32 ipfsMetadata`. `submitClaim` has several guards. The requested amount must not exceed the cover amount, and the claim must be filed after the cover start, so buying and claiming in one block is impossible. It must be filed before start + period + grace period. **Only one live claim per cover** is allowed, and filing requires a 0.05 ETH deposit ([Claims.sol](https://github.com/NexusMutual/smart-contracts/blob/master/contracts/modules/assessment/Claims.sol)). Sherlock enforces the same single-claim rule with a `ClaimActive` error ([ISherlockClaimManager.sol](https://github.com/sherlock-protocol/sherlock-v2-core/blob/HEAD/contracts/interfaces/managers/ISherlockClaimManager.sol)). For MUTAV the equivalent invariant is **at most one open provision per guarantee leg**, which prevents double provisioning at almost no cost.

Payout ordering is just as useful. Nexus's `redeemClaimPayout` sets the redeemed flag first, then calls `burnStake`, which shrinks both the allocation and the global active total by exactly the payout. Only then does `sendPayout` transfer the funds and force a recompute of the requirement ([Claims.sol](https://github.com/NexusMutual/smart-contracts/blob/master/contracts/modules/assessment/Claims.sol); [Cover.sol](https://github.com/NexusMutual/smart-contracts/blob/master/contracts/modules/cover/Cover.sol)). The remaining cover stays active after a partial claim ([Nexus docs: Cover](https://docs.nexusmutual.io/protocol/cover/)). In MUTAV terms, a claim payment reduces the guarantee leg, releases the matching provision, records the amount paid and re-evaluates the reserve in one instruction. Following the repo rule, it is never blocked by the coverage gate.

How claims are decided has shifted toward named, accountable people. Nexus moved through three models. From 2019 to 2023, any member could stake to vote, with a 70% threshold. From 2023 to November 2025, votes were stake-weighted majority. Since November 2025, a **three-person Claims Committee** decides by 2-of-3 majority and must give a written rationale. The voting window is 72 hours, extendable, and a 24-hour cooldown follows during which the Advisory Board can reverse a fraudulent vote. Payouts must be redeemed within 30 days, and denied claimants can appeal or refile. "Expert assessors have decided every claim since November 2025" ([Nexus docs: Claims Assessment](https://docs.nexusmutual.io/protocol/claims-assessment)). Sherlock used a 4-of-7 committee and allowed escalation to UMA's optimistic oracle, at a cost of about 22.2k USDC to the claimant. It added an emergency halt operator because an escalated decision was otherwise unstoppable ([ISherlockClaimManager.sol](https://github.com/sherlock-protocol/sherlock-v2-core/blob/HEAD/contracts/interfaces/managers/ISherlockClaimManager.sol)). Parametric triggers suit price events. InsurAce paid about $11–12M on a 10-day UST TWAP below $0.88 ([Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/insurace-says-it-will-pay-millions-to-claimants-after-terra-s-collapse)). They carry basis risk ([arXiv 2412.05321](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.05321v2)), however, and a lease default is a discretionary, evidence-based loss. The model that fits MUTAV is the committee one: operator files, a separate role decides within a fixed window, the record keeps who decided and when, and a compensating transition replaces any edit.

Credit registries add the half that cover protocols lack, which is **loss recognition before final settlement, and its reversal**. Maple's `impairLoan` adds principal plus net interest to `unrealizedLosses` and stops interest accruing. `removeLoanImpairment` is allowed only before the original due date. A pool delegate **cannot remove an impairment that the governor triggered** (`LM:RLI:NO_AUTH`) ([LoanManager.sol](https://github.com/maple-labs/fixed-term-loan-manager/blob/main/contracts/LoanManager.sol)). Centrifuge's permissionless `write_off` "will only take effect if it writes down more … This action will never writes up." Only a loan admin can write up, and once a policy is installed, only as far as the policy allows ([lib.rs](https://github.com/centrifuge/centrifuge-chain/blob/main/pallets/loans/src/lib.rs)). Goldfinch's permissionless `writedown(tokenId)` recomputes from lateness and applies only the *delta* against the stored `writedownsByPoolToken`. Repeated calls are therefore idempotent, and the writedown reverses as repayments arrive ([SeniorPool.sol](https://github.com/goldfinch-eng/mono/blob/main/packages/protocol/contracts/protocol/core/SeniorPool.sol)). Huma makes default a one-shot transition and sends any later payment through a separate `distributeLossRecovery` path, because "it is possible for the borrower to payback even after default" ([Credit.sol](https://github.com/00labs/huma-contracts-v2/blob/main/contracts/credit/Credit.sol); [CreditManager.sol](https://github.com/00labs/huma-contracts-v2/blob/main/contracts/credit/CreditManager.sol)). In MUTAV's vocabulary, provisions should be stored per record and changed only by delta. The operator raises them freely, and releases them only through claim closure or an admin co-signature. A provision forced by the reserve admin is one the operator cannot release. Money recovered from the tenant after a claim payment (a guarantor's subrogation recovery) gets its own record type, so reserve accounting can reverse the loss without rewriting history.

Maple's legal terms add a rule for the reserve's depositors that MUTAV should state openly. Lenders who withdraw during an impairment "will be unable to claim any returned capital in the event that there is restitution", and "any future recoveries will be pro-rated to those lenders that remained in the pool" ([Maple docs](https://docs.maple.finance/legal/syrupusdc-and-syrupusdt-defaults-and-impairments.md)). Recognising losses early is pointless if depositors can exit at a price set before the provision. Equally, whoever stays and bears a provision should receive the recoveries.

## Failures came from governance, keys and adoption, not from ledgers

The research found **no documented case of a cover protocol failing because its on-chain exposure aggregate was wrong**. Failures came from four other places.

The first is changing the terms after the event. In May 2022, InsurAce cut the claim window for Terra-related covers from 15 to 7 days in the middle of the event, and community members suspected it was trying to limit payouts ([Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/insurace-says-it-will-pay-millions-to-claimants-after-terra-s-collapse)). A single interested Substack author alleges that Unslashed uploaded a waiting-period rule after a claim had been filed, and that a Kleros subcourt was captured to deny it ([deepfivalue](https://deepfivalue.substack.com/p/the-kleros-experiment-has-failed)). This is unverified, and no response from either party was found. The remedy is structural: freeze each guarantee's terms by a hash at issuance, and make claim rules a versioned parameter that applies only to guarantees issued after the change.

The second is keys and bundled roles. In December 2020 a tampered browser extension drained about **$8M of NXM** from a Nexus founder's personal wallet, despite a hardware wallet. Nexus said members were not at risk ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/news/defi/2020-12-14-nexus-mutual-founder-attack-funds-8-million-lost-87632)). The August 2025 CrediX hack, on Sonic (an EVM chain) and unrelated to Credix on Solana, is starker. An attacker address had been added to the multisig six days earlier and given admin and bridge rights. One account holding POOL_ADMIN, BRIDGE, ASSET_LISTING_ADMIN, EMERGENCY_ADMIN and RISK_ADMIN together drained about **$4.5M** ([Halborn](https://www.halborn.com/blog/post/explained-the-credix-hack-august-2025)). MUTAV's split between the operator, which writes records, and the reserve admin, a separate Squads multisig, is the right answer. The lesson is to keep those roles unbundled as the program grows. That means separate keys for emergency pause, parameter changes and upgrades, and a timelock on adding signers. Centrifuge V3 shows a model for parameter changes. Changes inside policy apply at once. Changes outside policy must first be scheduled through `initiateAuthorization`, wait out a delay set by the policy, and can be cancelled; installing a new policy voids every pending authorization ([centrifuge/protocol hub README](https://github.com/centrifuge/protocol/blob/main/src/core/hub/README.md)). None of the protocols reviewed caps operator write volume on-chain. A per-window count and amount ceiling held in the state account is an inferred addition, but a cheap one, and it bounds the damage a compromised operator key can do.

The third is products dying with positions still live. Sherlock v1 and v2 had been unsupported since 2024 and were formally deprecated on 2026-09-10, with users told to withdraw funds still held in the contracts ([Sherlock](https://docs.sherlock.xyz/coverage/sherlock-v1-and-v2-deprecation-notice.md)). Neptune Mutual's docs no longer resolve. Goldfinch reportedly shut down its credit platform after the Tugende, Stratos and Lend East losses; dates conflict across sources ([The Defiant](https://thedefiant.io/news/defi/goldfinch-writes-down-failed-usd5m-loan-to-kenyan-taxi-financier); [Cryptopolitan](https://www.cryptopolitan.com/goldfinch-shuts-down-credit-platform/)). Credix's on-chain market is described as effectively defunct, at about $53K TVL, after the company pivoted to Brazilian SME lending through FIDCs ([Spark](https://www.spark.money/research/tokenized-private-credit-onchain); [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-11-06/btg-patria-among-investors-in-93-million-credix-funding-round)). A guarantee registry needs a defined run-off mode. New guarantees stop, but filings and claim payments on live guarantees continue until the last one expires.

The fourth is credit losses, which reshaped loss recognition rather than registries. Orthogonal Trading's default of about $36M led Maple v2 to add early default and impairment ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/amp/post/194907/maple-finance-releases-version-2-0-after-36-million-default-on-platform)). TrueFi's $3.4M Blockwater loan went to a court process after an off-chain restructuring ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/175843/south-korean-firm-blockwater-defaults-on-3-million-loan-from-truefi)). Goldfinch wrote down Tugende's $5M loan, an estimated 3.95% of NAV ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/09/14/tugende-goldfinch-loan/)). Every recovery happened off-chain and came back through an on-chain reversal path. For permissioned bank networks, the cause of death was adoption: we.trade went insolvent in 2022, Marco Polo in 2023 with about €5.2M of debts, and Contour stopped operating in November 2023 after failing to attract users. Only Komgo survived ([Tech Monitor](https://www.techmonitor.ai/technology/emerging-technology/ibm-backed-blockchain-platform-we-trade-shutting-down); [FinTech Futures](https://www.fintechfutures.com/blockchain-crypto-digital-assets/blockchain-trade-finance-firm-marco-polo-reportedly-enters-insolvency); [Ledger Insights](https://www.ledgerinsights.com/contour-blockchain-trade-finance-network-shutter/)). A single-operator protocol that distributes through agencies avoids the coordination problem that killed the consortia. It still has to win the same adoption.

## Privacy means opaque ids, keyed commitments and no property references

Every serious precedent keeps identities and documents off-chain. Huma's receivable NFT is "a transparency layer for the receivables" keyed by a hash of the reference, not the invoice itself ([Receivable.sol](https://github.com/00labs/huma-contracts-v2/blob/main/contracts/credit/Receivable.sol)). Centrifuge references collateral as an NFT `(collection, item)` and keeps documents off-chain ([entities/loans.rs](https://github.com/centrifuge/centrifuge-chain/blob/main/pallets/loans/src/entities/loans.rs)). Keyhold puts only a random lease id and a sha256 on-chain, and uses an allow-list so that nothing else can be written into a memo ([Keyhold](https://github.com/Malejo01/keyhold)). Regulators now expect the same. The EDPB's blockchain guidelines 02/2025 (final v2.0 reportedly adopted on 7 July 2026) discourage storing personal data on-chain and recommend commitments, keyed hashes, off-chain storage, and erasure by deleting the off-chain data or destroying the keys. **Unsalted hashes are generally insufficient** ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/edpb-forces-blockchain-firms-to-avoid-storing-personal-data-on-chain/); [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/fin-tech/1617044/blockchain-and-gdpr-edpb-guidelines-022025-adopted)). Industry argued that deleting the salt amounts to "functional erasure", and it is unconfirmed whether the final text accepts that ([GBBC reply](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/sites/default/files/webform/public_consultation_reply/gbbc-edpb-draft-guidelines-02_2025.pdf)). No ANPD guidance on blockchain was found. Brazilian academic work recommends the same pattern: personal data off-chain, a hash on-chain, and physical deletion off-chain while the audit trail stays intact ([UnB 2026](https://ppee.unb.br/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/Dissertacao_final-Joao-paulo.pdf); [SBC WBlockchain](https://sol.sbc.org.br/index.php/wblockchain/article/download/12435/12300)). Following the EDPB is therefore the conservative reading of LGPD.

MUTAV's design (opaque ids, salted or keyed hashes, reserve-math figures only, personal data held by the operator) already matches this baseline. Three refinements follow from the sources. First, never derive a seed or hash from a low-entropy identifier without a key. There are only about 10^11 valid CPFs, so an unsalted hash of one can be brute-forced; a per-record random salt or an operator-held HMAC key closes that gap. Second, the commitment recipe `H(version || canonical_document_bytes || salt)` supports selective disclosure: an agency reveals the document and salt to an auditor or court, who recomputes the hash and checks it against the PDA. Third, amounts in the clear are still metadata. A per-lease guarantee amount plus an agency wallet plus a date could point to a listing. Bucketing amounts or disclosing at agency level are possible mitigations that no precedent has tested. Token-2022 confidential transfers do not help here: they hide balances, not registry fields ([Solana post-mortem](https://solana.com/news/post-mortem-june-25-2025)).

Brazil adds one constraint that is specific to its law: **keep property out of the record entirely**. The ONR's president says "the owner is whoever is on the title record". CGJ-SP Provimento 54/2025 bars registrars from linking a title record (matrícula) to a token, and TJRS suspended its pro-tokenization rule in October 2025 ([ConJur](https://conjur.com.br/2026-mai-18/tokenizacao-e-o-registro-de-imoveis-provimento-no-54-25-da-cgj-sp-2/); [RIB](https://www.registrodeimoveis.org.br/tjrssuspendenormasobretokenizaaao)). A guarantee keyed by an opaque lease id, with no property reference, stays clear of that dispute. Liqi's tokenized Nigri–Porsche lease cash flow, followed by its pause after a CVM circular, also shows that **lease-linked tokens offered to investors are read as securities** ([Blocknews](https://www.blocknews.com.br/regulacao-governos/liqi-decide-paralisar-oferta-de-tokens-de-recebiveis-e-buscara-dialogo-com-cvm/)). That is a reason to keep guarantee records non-transferable accounts rather than NFTs, unlike the Nexus cover token. For attested off-chain events, the Solana Attestation Service offers credential → schema → attestation PDAs with authorised signers, expiry and pause. A registry write can require an unexpired attestation, such as `NoticeValidated{notice_hash}`, signed by a second party ([SAS GitHub](https://github.com/solana-foundation/solana-attestation-service)). SAS attestations can be closed, and the repo states no audit status. A claim record should therefore copy the attester and attestation key at write time. Audit-trail events should use `emit_cpi!`, because RPC providers can truncate logs; accounts remain the source of truth ([Solana docs: Anchor events](https://solana.com/docs/programs/anchor-events)).

## What MUTAV should adopt and avoid

| Adopt | Precedent | Avoid | Cautionary case |
|---|---|---|---|
| One guarantee record with a sub-amount per leg, each with its own provision and claim payment counters | Nexus `PoolAllocation[]`, Etherisc `openClaimsCount`/`payoutAmount` | Amendments that edit amounts in place without a delta to cached totals | Inferred drift source |
| Append-only amendments: a new record linked to the original and latest | Nexus `CoverReference` | Closing records so seeds can be reused | Anchor revival attacks |
| Totals updated in the same instruction; unsmoothed gate; read-only `verify_totals` crank | Nexus `_updateTotalActiveCoverAmount`, Etherisc locked capital | Time-smoothing the coverage-gate input | Nexus 500 bps/day cap is right for a price, wrong for a gate |
| One open provision per guarantee leg; filing after start and within window plus grace | Nexus `submitClaim`, Sherlock `ClaimActive` | Rules changed after issuance | InsurAce window cut; Unslashed allegation |
| Provisions only rise under the operator; releases need claim closure or the admin; admin-forced provisions cannot be released by the operator | Centrifuge write-off, Maple `NO_AUTH` | Losses recognised only at final payment | Maple 2022 led to impairment |
| Separate recovery record type that reverses losses | Huma `distributeLossRecovery`, Goldfinch delta writedown | Rewriting history to reflect recoveries | Every credit recovery happened off-chain |
| Decider role separate from filer, with recorded rationale hash, decider and timestamps, and a time-boxed override | Nexus Claims Committee (Nov 2025) | Token-vote or anonymous-juror claim decisions | Nexus moved away from them; Kleros allegation |
| Unbundled roles, timelocked out-of-policy changes, per-window operator caps | Centrifuge V3 authorizations | One key holding admin, emergency and bridge roles | CrediX $4.5M |
| A defined run-off mode for live guarantees | Sherlock deprecation (by contrast) | Wind-down without a claim path | Sherlock, Goldfinch, Credix |
| Keyed or salted commitments, opaque ids, no property references, non-transferable records | Huma `referenceId`, Keyhold, EDPB 02/2025 | Unsalted hashes of CPF or contract numbers; property title links; tradeable guarantee tokens | EDPB; CGJ-SP 54/2025; CVM on Liqi |

## Conclusion

The research changes where MUTAV's novelty lies. The registry mechanics are not new. Per-record state machines, cached aggregates and monotone loss recognition have run in production for years across cover and credit protocols. What no one has done is put them together on a public chain for a lease guarantee: exposure that drives capital, as at Nexus; provisions that count before payment, as Maple's impairment does; and a lifecycle like Lygon's bank-guarantee ledger. Counting filed-claim provisions in coverage_required already makes MUTAV's gate stricter than the best-known cover protocol. The remaining work is mostly governance hardening. That means terms frozen at issuance, a decider separate from the filer, unbundled and timelocked authority, and a run-off path.

The open risks lie outside the program. Whether a public per-lease amount can identify a tenant is untested, and ANPD has said nothing on hashes on public chains. Lygon's fate, and whether any Brazilian legal registry will need to mirror MUTAV's records, are unknown. The strongest signal from the failure record is that registries rarely broke. Trust broke when someone could change the rules, the keys or the product underneath live positions. A small, typed and auditable registry is therefore necessary, but the governance around it is what will earn trust in it.
