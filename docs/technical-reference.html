1Overview#

MUTAV is an institutional fiador for Brazilian rental leases. The mutav program holds a BRL reserve in BRS (a BRL token issued by Nora) and records every guarantee MUTAV gives, with its remaining cover. It mints reserve shares to allowlisted capital providers at an on-chain NAV, and it gates every movement of capital and every new guarantee on a solvency check against the registered cover. Claim payments are never solvency-gated: within admin-set caps they always go through. The program keeps internal accounting, so tokens sent to it do not move NAV until an authorised instruction books them. It prices nothing, decides no claim and holds no personal data: those duties stay in the MUTAV platform, which acts on-chain through the operator key.

Architecture in one line. Reserve admin (Squads multisig), operator key, pauser and guardians sign instructions to mutav; one VaultConfig per reserve mint; a vault authority PDA owns every reserve token account and the share mint; clients compose unsigned transactions through @mutav-finance/mutav-protocol-solana.

3Roles & permissions#

The operator and the reserve admin are separate parties and may be separate companies. The split is the core security rule.

RoleOwnsKey
OperatorContracts, agencies and the whole guarantee and claim cycle. Holds the information: registers, closes and reinstates guarantees; books guarantee fees and issuer income; files, withdraws and pays claims; records settlement. Every registry action is reversible by another operator action. Only pay_claim is irreversible, and it is bounded by admin caps.Hot key
Reserve adminFinancial health and security through ceilings, not contract judgment: caps, coverage ratio, stress buffer, roles, accounts, allowlist, fills of the capital queues, approval of claim amounts above the per-call cap, unsolicited money, unpause.Admin multisig vault
PauserEmergency stop. pause and revoke_operator, with no time lock.Separate key
Guardianspause only. Cannot revoke, unpause or change anything.Up to 3 keys
Investor (owner)Own requests: deposit, redeem, cancel, claim. Entry is allowlisted; exit is open to any wallet that already holds shares.Own wallet
AnyonePermissionless cranks that need no judgment: refresh, advance_queue_heads, skim.Any signer

The maximum financial loss from a compromised operator is the admin-set claim caps. Every other change an operator key can make is reversible.

5Instructions#

Common rules. Every instruction refuses an account whose version is above 1 or whose status is unknown (UnsupportedVersion). Every BRS transfer uses transfer_checked with the stored mint and token program. Investor debits require the token account's owner == signer; delegates are never used. Math is u128, checked, rounded in the reserve's favour (MathOverflow on overflow). new marks an instruction added by the 2026-10-10 decisions; changed marks one whose rules changed.

6Economics & math#

All amounts are BRS base units; BRS is valued at par with BRL. Products use u128. Rounding is always in the reserve's favour: down for what it pays or mints, up for what it requires or is owed.

6.1 Solvency quantities#

stable_assets         = brs_balance + priced_asset_value          // tracked balances only; 0 for a frozen reserve
                        // excludes pending_deposits_total and claimable_assets_total
remaining_cover(g)    = (default_cover − default_paid) + (exit_cover − exit_paid)
remaining_cover_total = Σ remaining_cover(g) over guarantees not CLOSED
unprovisioned_cover   = remaining_cover_total − provisions
coverage_required     = max( ceil(c × remaining_cover_total / 10,000),
                             provisions + min(stress_buffer, unprovisioned_cover) )
surplus               = max(0, stable_assets − coverage_required)
free_capital          = surplus − earmark_eff                     // earmark_eff = 0 in v1
liquid_budget         = max(0, brs_balance − provisions − earmark_eff)
net_assets            = max(0, stable_assets − provisions)

Stress buffer. Claims arrive per event: one month's rent per default claim, one exit payment at the end. Stress is the claims that could be filed next, not every full cover. The admin sizes stress_buffer from the portfolio (for example the three largest rents plus one exit; about R$19k on devnet) and resizes it with set_config. It gates new guarantees and redemptions. It never gates a claim payment.

Solvency gate. register_guarantee needs coverage_required_after ≤ stable_assets and mode normal. fulfil_redeems needs mode normal and each fill within min(free_capital, liquid_budget). The gate never applies to file_claim, pay_claim, withdraw_claim, settle_payout, contribute_fees, sweep_income, close_guarantee, cancels, claims or cranks. Mode is UNDER_COVERED when stable_assets < coverage_required.

6.2 NAV and share pricing#

V                  = 1                                                  // virtual offset 10^0
shares_for(assets) = floor(assets × (shares_outstanding + V) / (net_assets + 1))   // deposit: down
assets_for(shares) = floor(shares × (net_assets + 1) / (shares_outstanding + V))   // redeem: down
conversion_nav     = floor((net_assets + 1) × 10^9 / (shares_outstanding + V))     // recorded per fill
published_nav      = 0                                          if shares_outstanding = 0
                   = max(1, floor(net_assets × 10^9 / shares_outstanding))  otherwise

Pending deposits and redemptions are excluded from net_assets. Escrowed redeem shares stay in shares_outstanding until a fill burns them. Pricing uses tracked balances only, so a direct transfer cannot inflate NAV. Inflows that raise NAV without minting shares (contribute_fees, sweep_income) are refused while no shares exist, so a first deposit always mints at a meaningful price; every fill must mint more than 0 shares. When net assets are 0 with shares outstanding, deposit fills are refused (ReserveUnderwater) so new money never pays for old losses; recovery is MUTAV backstop BRS booked by the admin, which mints no shares.

The claim window keeps two buckets of length claim_period_secs: the current one and the previous one. A payment is checked against the current bucket plus the previous bucket weighted by the share of it still inside the trailing window:

elapsed   = now − claim_period_start                     // after rolling buckets
estimate  = prev_paid × (period − elapsed) / period + current_paid     // rounded up
require     estimate + amount ≤ max_claim_per_period

The design goal is that no 30-day span exceeds max_claim_per_period, removing the tumbling window's "twice the cap at the boundary" effect. Exact rounding is pinned at implementation.

6.4 NAV-move guard#

inflow_nav  += ceil(net × 10^9 / shares_outstanding)      // at each contribute_fees, sweep_income, booking
guard_nav    = max(1, published_nav − inflow_nav)        // 0 if no shares
halt if      |guard_nav − baseline| × 10,000 > max_nav_move_bps × baseline   (baseline > 0, guard_nav > 0)

Measured per share, so fills in the same window do not distort it. Evaluated in refresh and inline in both fulfil instructions. The halt is sticky; only clear_fulfil_halt clears it and resets the baseline. A drift below the bound across many refreshes is not caught (see §13).

6.5 Fee take and income intake#

fee:    take = floor(amount × fee_take_bps / 10,000)      → treasury_account
        net  = amount − take                              → reserve   (NAV rises, no shares)
income: take = floor(amount × income_take_bps / 10,000)   = 0 in v1
        net  = amount − take                              → reserve   (receipt kind NORA_STATEMENT)

Issuer income is the issuer's partnership revenue paid into the income inbox at an off-chain contractual rate. BRS itself bears no yield. Income is never a guarantee fee and never enters fees_in_total.

7Lifecycles#

7.1 Guarantee#

  • Register (operator): solvency-gated, capped per guarantee and per agency. Cover counts in remaining_cover_total at once.
  • Close (operator): VOID or RELEASED; requires no open claim. Releases remaining cover.
  • Reinstate (operator): undoes a close, for example one made by a compromised key.
  • The account is never closed, so the public claims history stays readable.

7.2 Claim#

  1. File: the operator books the provision. NAV drops at once.
  2. Approve (only above max_claim_per_call): the admin records approved_amount, raising the period cap in the same proposal if needed.
  3. Pay: the operator pays in one call to the payments account. Never refused for solvency, mode, pause or the NAV guard.
  4. Settle: the operator records the Pix hash after MUTAV pays the agency.
  5. Withdraw (instead of pay): the operator releases the provision; the notice can be filed again.

Bank-first payments. MUTAV may pay the agency by Pix from its own bank account first; the reserve then reimburses the payments account through pay_claim. This keeps payments on time while an above-cap approval waits on the time lock.

7.3 Deposit and redemption queues#

  • Each request is its own PDA at a monotonically increasing seq. Seqs are never reused.
  • The admin fills in strict FIFO order at the NAV of the fill. Up to 8 per call. Concurrent requests may race on the seq and need a client retry.
  • Whole fills only in v1. A head larger than the budget blocks the queue until free capital grows. Demo request sizes stay below free capital, and the consoles explain a stuck head (needed vs available).
  • Owners cancel or claim at any time, also under pause. advance_queue_heads clears dead seqs for free.
  • Entry is allowlisted. Exit is open to any holder, and a full exit may be below min_request. A pending deposit from a de-listed wallet is returned by refund_deposit.
  • MUTAV's own capital uses the same queues with no special treatment.

7.4 Unsolicited money and backstop#

  1. Money arrives outside a booking path: into a tracked account (skimmed by anyone) or the income inbox outside a statement (classified by the admin). It lands in unsolicited.
  2. Runbook: screen the source. If the sender is identified and asks within 30 days, the admin returns it to the original source. Otherwise the admin books it (kind UNSOLICITED). Dust below a threshold (for example R$10) is booked directly.
  3. Foreign classic SPL tokens and excess SOL are returned with return_foreign. They are never listed in the app.
  4. Backstop: MUTAV sends BRS to recapitalise an underwater reserve; the admin books it with kind BACKSTOP. No shares are minted, so the money raises NAV for every holder.

8Invariants#

Asserted in tests after every instruction unless noted.

  1. stable_assets uses only tracked balances (and a bounded price for a priced asset), never raw token balances.
  2. Per guarantee: leg_paid ≤ leg_cover and leg_provision ≤ leg_cover − leg_paid on both legs, with any number of open filings.
  3. remaining_cover_total = Σ remaining cover over non-closed guarantees; provisions = Σ provision of FILED filings.
  4. Token balances are at least the tracked totals: reserve ≥ brs_balance, pending_deposits ≥ pending_deposits_total, claims ≥ claimable_assets_total, pending_redemptions ≥ pending_redeem_shares.
  5. Share supply + shares owed on filled, unclaimed deposits = shares_outstanding.
  6. coverage_required ≥ provisions. A provision lowers NAV only, never stable_assets.
  7. The solvency gate never refuses pay_claim. Only the filing checks, the cover bound, the caps, liquid BRS or a frozen reserve can stop it.
  8. A claim payment cannot fail on the cached coverage_required write (saturating).
  9. Total claim payments in any trailing claim_period_secs stay within max_claim_per_period (design goal of the sliding window), approved payments included.
  10. A payment above max_claim_per_call requires an admin approved_amount on that filing.
  11. The maximum loss from a compromised operator is bounded by the claim caps. Every other operator change is reversible by another operator action.
  12. Gated actions (register_guarantee, each redemption fill) fit in the free capital computed before them.
  13. Every fill is priced at the NAV of that fill and rounds in the reserve's favour, so it never lowers NAV per share.
  14. Strict FIFO: no request is filled while an earlier one still has shares remaining. v1 fills whole requests.
  15. Σ shares_remaining over open redeem requests = pending_redeem_shares; Σ assets_claimable = claimable_assets_total.
  16. Every deposit fill mints more than 0 shares.
  17. No inflow raises NAV while shares_outstanding == 0.
  18. No deposit fill while net assets are 0 and shares are outstanding.
  19. NAV rises from issuer income only through sweep_income, and from unsolicited or backstop money only through an admin booking, each by exactly its net.
  20. With no outside transfers, reserve.amount == brs_balance after any instruction sequence.
  21. inflow_nav = Σ ceil(net × 10^9 / shares_outstanding) over inflows since the last refresh or halt clear, saturating.
  22. BRS leaves the reserve's accounts only to: the payments account, the treasury (fee take), the claims escrow and on to its owner, the owner of a returned pending deposit, or (from unsolicited only) the original sender.
  23. The income inbox's exits are reserve, the treasury (take, 0 in v1) and unsolicited. Income is never returned.
  24. A frozen reserve counts as 0 BRS in every solvency computation that reads it.
  25. Roles are distinct keys. A role changes only when the new key signs its acceptance; revocation is immediate.
  26. Covers never change after registration.
  27. _reserved bytes are never read or written by logic and survive every instruction.

9.1 Pause scope#

Stopped by pauseAlways open

Pause never traps holders' money and never blocks a claim. Who may pause: pauser, guardians, admin, with no time lock. Only the admin unpauses or clears a NAV halt.

9.2 Frozen reserve#

The issuer can freeze any BRS account. A frozen reserve counts as 0 BRS in refresh, register_guarantee and clear_fulfil_halt; register_guarantee, fills, skim and clear_fulfil_halt refuse with ReserveFrozen; refresh emits ReserveFrozenDetected. A freeze also blocks pay_claim; MUTAV then pays by Pix from its own bank and is reimbursed after. The reserve's money is split across five token accounts so one freeze does not trap everything.

9.3 Operator-compromise runbook#

  1. Freeze. The pauser runs revoke_operator at once, and pause if needed. Operator instructions fail from the next slot.
  2. Appoint. The admin proposes a new operator with propose_roles; the new key accepts with accept_role. If the pauser key is also suspect, replace it in the same proposal.
  3. Reconcile. The new operator compares the platform's contract registry with on-chain events since the compromise.
  4. Correct with ordinary operator actions. Fake registrations: close_guarantee(VOID). Wrongful closes: reinstate_guarantee. Fake filings: withdraw_claim. No admin rollback instruction and no time-window argument exist.
  5. Accept the bounded loss. Claim payments made by the attacker are irreversible. They are bounded by the caps and landed in MUTAV's own payments account.
  6. The admin signs no redemption fill until reconciliation is done.

Pauser compromise. A stolen pauser key can stop claim payments by revoking the operator until the admin's time lock lets a new propose_roles through. Runbook: replace the pauser in the same proposal; bank-first payment covers the gap.

Early detection. The app shows an operator-activity feed (registrations, closes, filings, payments in the last 24 hours).

12Upgrade rules & layout freeze#

12.1 Two multisigs#

MultisigHoldsTime lockDevnet

Both multisigs must have no config authority, so members, threshold and time lock change only through their own proposals. The Squads time lock counts from approval. Real keys (hardware wallets) replace the devnet stand-ins before real money. The deploy scripts verify the exact member set, the threshold and the Squads v4 owner, and refuse any cluster other than devnet by genesis hash.

12.2 Layout rules#

  • R1. First fields version, bump. R1b. A version above the binary's, or an unknown status, is refused.
  • R2. Fixed-size types only. Statuses are u8 constants. No Option, Vec, String or data enums.
  • R3. Carved fields read zero as the safe default; flags are u8 or bits, never bool.
  • R4. _reserved is last, zeroed, never touched by logic; fields are carved from its front.
  • R5. Append-only: never reorder, retype or delete; a retired field becomes _deprecated_<name>; never rename an account struct.
  • R6. Update in place, so bytes written by a newer binary survive a rollback.
  • R7. Sizes pinned with a compile-time assertion per account.
  • R8. Large accounts are boxed in instruction contexts.

12.3 The layout freeze#

The pre-freeze edits land before the first devnet deploy: the larger padding of Guarantee, ClaimFiling and Payout, removal of the payout SLA fields, the account-list changes (seeds-checked reserve in register_guarantee and clear_fulfil_halt), the reason argument of close_guarantee, and the new instructions and carves. Golden layout fixtures (tests/fixtures/layout/v1/) are regenerated once more for these edits. From the deploy on, v1 is frozen: every change is a carve from padding or a new account, checked against the v1 fixtures.

12.5 Upgrade runbook (summary)#

  1. ADR and spec update in the same PR; no diff in the pay_claim path; layout, padding-preservation, idl-compat and full test suites green.
  2. Rehearse on a fork of the live cluster against real accounts, then on devnet with the same threshold and time lock.
  3. Verifiable build; buffer authority set to the upgrade vault; buffer hash equals build hash.
  4. One Squads vault transaction: ExtendProgramChecked first if needed, then Upgrade, the IDL write and the verify record. Each signer reproduces the hash and decodes every instruction; any SetAuthority is a reason to reject.
  5. Execute after the time lock; verify the on-chain hash; smoke-check that carved fields read zero.
  6. Optional features are enabled later in a separate time-locked set_config.

13Security reviews & known limitations#

13.1 Reviews to date#

Pre-devnet adversarial review (2026-10-09). Five parallel read-only reviews of the program (account checks and roles; economics and math; queues, lifecycle and layout), the client and scripts, and the pilot app. Findings were re-checked against the code and each came with a proposed fix. The main themes, all addressed by the design on this page:

  • Account padding too small for the planned lifecycle fields: grown before the freeze.
  • Claim payments above the per-call cap: now an explicit admin approval inside a strict period cap.
  • No way to reverse a wrongful filing or close: withdraw_claim and reinstate_guarantee, under the operator/admin separation.
  • Share precision when inflows precede the first deposit: inflows gated on outstanding shares; zero-share fills refused.
  • NAV guard only in refresh: now also inline in both fulfils.
  • De-listed holders and dust: exit open to holders, full exits below the minimum, admin refunds of pending deposits.
  • Tumbling claim window allowing about twice the cap at a boundary: sliding window.
  • Frozen reserve ignored by registration: seeds-checked reserve, fail closed.
  • Deploy-script and app hardening (cluster checks by genesis hash, exact multisig checks, decimals checks, relay and headers): handled outside the program.

Whole-protocol review (2026-10-10). 59 findings from three published audits of a comparable Solana program were mapped to MUTAV by vulnerability class; nearly all were sound, not applicable or already known. Adopted from that comparison: two-step role and admin handover, pause-only guardians, two multisigs, and keeping owner cancels and claims open under pause.

Confirmed sound in review. The solvency gate never refuses pay_claim. Account validation is tight (seeds and canonical bumps, owners, mints, token program, has_one, no re-initialisation). initialize cannot be front-run. Pause scope matches the design. Math is checked u128 and rounds in the reserve's favour. Pricing uses tracked balances only, so donations cannot inflate NAV. sweep_income validates the inbox and post-CPI deltas and is idempotent per receipt. The client math mirror is exact and the client holds no signing code.

Next. A simplification pass and an independent security audit precede the devnet deploy. The program is unaudited.
